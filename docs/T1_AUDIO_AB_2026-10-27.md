The two input pitch sequences are identical. Relative dominant spectral peaks

Spectral estimates were used relatively, not to judge absolute oscillator tuning.

## Recorded onset alignment

At 124 BPM / 48 kHz, the primary detector compares five milliseconds of audio
energy before/after each candidate onset, using 0.5 ms bins and a ±35 ms search
around each recorded input-MIDI note. Both clips have straight tempo-matched
warp markers, zero source offset and zero loop start; no manual warping was done.

| Condition | Median offset | 5th–95th percentile | Range |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Green / On | +0.52 ms | −0.41 to +0.88 ms | −1.63 to +0.90 ms |
| Red / Off | +0.58 ms | −0.09 to +0.98 ms | −0.71 to +1.04 ms |

The approximately −0.06 ms median difference is smaller than detector resolution
and must not be described as the quantizer making audio faster. Eight- and ten-
millisecond energy windows give On medians +0.60/+0.60 ms versus Off +0.58/+0.63
ms: the practical conclusion is unchanged. Envelope, pitch and oscillator phase
affect attack estimates; this is not sub-sample laboratory timing.

**Conclusion:** no measurable added quantizer delay or meaningful timing
degradation is evident in these two hardware-audio passes. The basic A/B check
passes; no additional take is required to establish this result.

## Boundaries

These values compare recorded audio against recorded MIDI after Live's capture
and compensation behavior. They are **not** T-1 physical-action → oscillator
latency, nor a separate FH-2 CV/gate timing measurement. They do not contradict
the earlier approximately 3.5 ms lowest measured software-MIDI path. This is two
short monophonic passes, not proof about long-session stress, every quantizer
mode, Hold or ensemble coordination windows. Independent audio-event counting
outside expected-note search windows was not performed.

in `tests/test_audio_ab.py`. Numerical evidence: `T1_AUDIO_AB_2026-10-27.json`.
